○ Is a photograph the proper subject of a photograph?

○ Neuro-cognitive dissonance. We perceive ordinary, photographic images without any cognitive difficulty. We don't ask ourselves “are the people in that photo really 3 inches tall?” Some out of scale images cause a perceptual hiccup. A photo of SX 70 film that is intentionally outsized creates a moment of incongruence - a bit of perceptual push-pull.
(I don't know what re-presenting these images 'small' for this website means, but it's problematic)

○ Photo as found object. "Found Object" as Dadaist Marcel Duchamp legitimized with his famous R. Mutt Urinal. In the case of photo as subject, the found object - the original 3 x 4 inch SX-70 film - is not posited as the art, but rather the photographic reference of the new image - where is the art? (if anywhere).

○ Self-reference is always interesting, e.g. "this sentence is false", “this page left intentionally blank”, our endless fascination with selfies, et al

If photo as means holds images in waiting to be made an end, this page holds <em>photos as ends in themselves</em>. They say what they say. They are a choice I made to present a view of the world and my worldview. 

I say without humility, that I have not put in the hours required to claim the title “Photographer.” I have <a href="https://www.harmelphoto.com/" class="frame-link">a friend who is a photographer</a> and I know the difference.  That said, I am pleased with the photos presented here (again, and obviously, said without humility :).
