In recent years, the photos I've taken, I call "art scrap." That is, I'm not creating a photograph as an end, but rather as an image in waiting – not knowing what idea might complete it.*

<span class="category-intro-footnote">*except <em>Network of Bales (after Duchamp)</em> when I saw the bales, I knew. see its faq</span>

[2]
reasons:

○ Is a photograph the proper subject of a photograph?

○ Photo as found object. Any "found object" can become art given intentionality, as Dadaist Marcel Duchamp legitimized with his famous R. Mutt urinal, and further that an image of art could become art - L.H.O.O.Q. 

○ Self-reference is always interesting: "this sentence is false", our endless fascination with selfies, etc.

○ Neuro-cognitive dissonance. We perceive ordinary photographic images without any cognitive difficulty. We don't ask ourselves “are the people in that photo really 3 inches tall?” Some out-of-scale images cause a processing hiccup - a bit of perceptual push-pull-push.

[3]
If photo as means holds images in waiting to be made an end, this section holds <em>photos as ends in themselves</em>. They are a choice I made to present a view of the world and my worldview. 

I say without humility, that I have not put in the hours required to claim the title “Photographer.” I have <a href="https://www.harmelphoto.com/" class="frame-link">a friend who is a photographer</a> and I know the difference.  That said, I am pleased with the photos presented here (also said without humility :)
