about Art
Mostly, I think of art in terms of Wonder: the sense of wonder engendered by any art — painting, music, film, photography, sculpture, dance, architecture … Art is all around us, all the time. Wonder is that experience of why? how? to what end? who? who for? — of awe, confusion, harmony, surprise, pleasure, suffering, bliss.

about Wonder
Wonder is a word to describe experiences that cannot be described in words. Wonder is that tiny, immense kernel of curiosity, of yearning, of indefinable will, that asks us, in some inexplicable way, to seek meaning, understanding, spirituality, relationships, and community. Wonder is the cause of art. Wonder is the effect of art. Wonder is necessarily, and intrinsically, present in art.

about AI
AI doesn’t wonder. I have loved science and technology my entire life, so, not surprisingly, I like AI, the mathematics that manifests it, and the mystery of how it all works. No doubt, AI’s capabilities are terrifying and wonder-full and will require us humans to make many serious moral choices. As for art, sorting out the role of AI is going to be messy and a lot of fun.

about My Art
In the popups below my art images, you’ll find a few words about what I was thinking when I made the piece, or in some cases, what the finished piece revealed to me about my thoughts. You may find these interesting, or not, just like the works themselves.
Explanations are often thought to steal or alter art-inspired wonder. This has not been my experience: wonder has been enhanced and extended in ways that, absent explanation, would not have been available to me. So I like words of explanation. I like words about the contradictory nature of understanding: words describing the ineffable.
All purportedly serious words aside, I think art is just fun. If you look at my work and have a bit of fun, that is more than enough for me.

about me
Below is my “standard” artist’s bio.
Maurice Konkle is a recommitted artist living in Fayetteville, Arkansas. His interest in art, photography, and filmmaking began over 40 years ago. While an undergraduate, he studied philosophy, mathematics, and physics, but spent much of his time making photographs and films. Later, while working with Polaroid SX-70 photos, he discovered his interest in photographic minimalism and hand-augmenting “finished” images. This visual development coincided with his time as a graduate student in Philosophy at the University of Washington, where he earned a Master of Arts degree.
After graduate school, his focus became business and family. He worked in several computer/technical arts companies, including as Executive Vice President of the pioneering computer database company, Cipher; as Executive Producer of award-winning television commercials at Celluloid Studios; and as COO of the internationally acclaimed video game company, Oddworld Inhabitants. His move to Fayetteville in 2016 afforded him the opportunity to return to his art practice. His work has been exhibited in galleries and public spaces in Northwest Arkansas.
